## The First Settlement Age — Years 18–63

### 1. Year 18 — The Founding of Orisward

After years of wandering the western waters, seven storm-battered clans entered the sheltered **Bay of Oris** [X: 06, Y: 17]. They raised the timber settlement of **Orisward** [X: 06, Y: 18] upon the bay’s southern lip, where ships could beach safely and freshwater streams descended from the high ground.

The settlers survived first by fishing, salt-making, and cutting timber from **Elderweald** [X: 07, Y: 18]. Oak became hull-planking; yew became bows; lesser wood fed smokehouses and lime kilns. Within a generation, the forest tiles at [X: 07, Y: 18], [X: 08, Y: 18], and [X: 09, Y: 18] had been felled. Stumps and charcoal pits replaced the old canopy, leaving open ground around **Orisward** [X: 06, Y: 18].

The clearing awakened no visible enemy, yet hunters began finding men hanging high in the remaining trees—without rope, wound, or footprint beneath them. Thus the innermost woodland was avoided, and no road was driven north into its shadow.

### 2. Years 41–63 — Vaelmark and the First Road

Population pressure sent families eastward from **Orisward** [X: 06, Y: 18]. Their wagons followed the newly cleared southern edge of **Elderweald** [X: 09, Y: 18] before reaching the fertile **Vaelplain** [X: 12, Y: 18].

In Year 41 they founded **Vaelmark** [X: 15, Y: 19] on a raised flood terrace beside the **River Vael** [X: 16, Y: 19]. The site offered black soil, freshwater, reeds, fish, and a defensible western bank. Earthen dikes were raised around the settlement, while shallow drainage channels were cut through the adjoining fields.

The **Oris–Vael Track** was completed in Year 63. It ran through [X: 07–15, Y: 18], then turned south into **Vaelmark** [X: 15, Y: 19]. Timber, salt, and dried fish travelled east; grain, flax, and cattle returned west.

At the river crossing, the settlers built **Vaelbridge** [X: 16, Y: 19], the first permanent bridge in Edrath. Its stone piers narrowed the channel, causing heavier spring floods downstream. The western fields prospered behind their dikes, but water increasingly gathered in the southern lowlands—a small wound in the land whose cost had not yet come due.
